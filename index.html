Ritlager

Draw with a pen tablet or a mouse directly on top of any webpage or Research Catalogue page and export the drawing (PNG, JPG, TIFF, GIF or video) to place back into the page. Ritlager is Swedish for “drawing layer”.

1. Make the bookmark

✎ Ritlager

Drag the button above up to your bookmarks bar (show the bar with Ctrl+Shift+B, or Cmd+Shift+B on Mac). If dragging does not work: press Copy below, create a new bookmark by hand and paste the text as its address (URL).

2. Use it

  1. Open the page you want to draw on: any webpage, or your exposition in Research Catalogue (view or edit mode).
  2. Click the bookmark. A drawing layer covers the whole page and a small toolbar appears.
  3. Draw with your tablet. D toggles between Draw and Click, so you can click around in RC between strokes.
  4. Export PNG gives you an image to upload to RC as an image object. Choose the background: transparent (default), white, a custom colour, or a screenshot of the page. With Image size: Whole page the PNG fits the page’s top-left corner, so the strokes land exactly where you drew them. Place it over or under other objects.
  5. Click the bookmark again to hide/show. Reload the page to start over.

Good to know

  • Tools: Pen, Brush, Line (with optional arrowhead) and Eraser. Shortcuts: B P L E, [ and ] change thickness, Ctrl+Z undoes.
  • Pen styles (for Pen): Round, Square, Flat (calligraphy), Pencil (grainy), Marker (transparent highlighter), Ink (tapering ends, small drops, soft bleed) and Watercolour (translucent wash with darker edges; use 15–40 px). Sharp–Soft blurs the edges like an airbrush.
  • Thickness: ten quick sizes from a 0.2 px hairline to 64 px, plus a slider (0.2–100 px). Thin lines has two modes: Smooth (never thinner than 1 px, so lines stay unbroken) and Black hairlines (truly thin, under 1 px, with less visible pressure variation).
  • Thickness goes up to 1000 px: use the slider, the round presets (up to 512), or type an exact number in the box.
  • Opacity and Contrast show their value in %. Contrast redraws thin or semi-transparent strokes several times so they get darker; it makes no difference to solid, thick strokes at 100% opacity, and does not affect Brush, Airbrush, text or pictures.
  • The first time, Ritlager starts with the black pen at thickness 4. After that it remembers your last settings (tool, colour, thickness and so on); the file type always starts as PNG.
  • When you finish a text (click outside it, Esc or Ctrl+Enter), Ritlager goes back to the tool you drew with before. Choose the Text tool again for the next text.
  • All menus start closed each time you start Ritlager on a page; click a name to open it.
  • Effects (its own menu): Dodge (lighter) and Burn (darker) are brushes that lighten or darken what you have already drawn on the active layer (never the page behind); Exposure sets the strength, size comes from Thickness and softness from the Airbrush sliders. Layer effects give the active layer a Blur, a coloured Glow and a Drop shadow; they apply to the whole layer, also in exports, and can be switched off again without losing anything. Retouch has four brushes that work on the pixels you have drawn on the active layer: Blur, Sharpen, Smudge (drags the paint along, like a finger in wet paint) and Clone (Alt+click where to copy from, then paint somewhere else; a red cross shows the source). Brush size (each of the four tools remembers its own), Strength, Amount (blur distance or sharpen amount) and Hardness set how they behave; Each stroke is kept as an edit, so Undo works.
  • File type and resolution (Export menu): save as PNG (sharp, can be transparent), JPG (small file, no transparency, so a transparent background becomes white; set the quality) or TIFF (uncompressed, with transparency, for print and archive). DPI starts at 72 for PNG/JPG (right for Research Catalogue, which only looks at pixels) and 300 for TIFF; it is stored in the file so Photoshop, InDesign and printers know the print size (the colours are always sRGB; convert to CMYK or another profile in your image program). Resolution 1×–6× makes the image larger than on screen, drawn sharply again at the larger size (not just stretched); the pixel size is shown under the setting. Copy image always gives PNG.
  • Animate (its own menu, between Layers and Export): make an animation of your drawing and save it as a GIF or a video (WebM). Replay my drawing makes frames that show the strokes being drawn one after another (choose how many frames); or draw something, press + Add current drawing as frame, change the drawing, add again, and so on for hand-made animation. The frames appear as small pictures: ◀ ▶ move a frame, ⧉ copies it, ✕ removes it, and Keep every 2nd halves the number. Preview plays it right there (if you have no frames yet it makes them from your drawing first; frames are not kept when you restart Ritlager), Time / frame sets the speed, Pause at end holds the last frame and Loop repeats it. Fade in and Fade out (in milliseconds) add a smooth fade from and to the background colour at the start and end, in the preview, the GIF and the video. With a transparent background the fade goes via white. Choose size (up to 1000 px wide), background (white, transparent or a colour) and whether to keep only the drawn area. A GIF has at most 255 colours, so soft airbrush and watercolour look a little coarser; the video is smoother and smaller. Whether Research Catalogue plays an uploaded GIF is worth testing with a small file; RC also takes video.
  • Picking a stroke: with ↖ Pick, click right on the line (a few pixels of margin) – the stroke under your pen is chosen, also when strokes cross or overlap. Click another stroke to switch even if it lies inside the box of the selected one.
  • Changing an existing stroke: your settings (Opacity, Colour, Thickness, Look, Pen / Brush / Airbrush) always set how the next stroke is drawn. They only change an old stroke when you have selected it: press ↖ Select (or S), click the stroke with Pick, drag a Rectangle or Lasso around it, or press Last stroke in the Select tool menu. While it is selected, changing a setting changes that stroke (and the controls show its current values). Ctrl+Z undoes the change. Nothing selected means nothing old is touched.
  • Airbrush (A): a soft spray that builds up as you go over the same place. Hardness sets how soft the edge is (0 = very soft, 100 = hard), Build-up how fast the colour gathers, and Opacity and Thickness work like for the other tools. It reacts to pen pressure and can be moved, resized and undone like any stroke.
  • Select tool (its own section, below the drawing tools): choose a mode, ↖ Pick (click one stroke, then drag it), ▭ Rectangle (also V) or Lasso (draw around the strokes you want), then drag around them. Select from chooses whether only the active layer or all visible layers can be selected. Drag inside the box to move, or drag a corner square to resize (proportions are kept; hold Shift to stretch freely). Ctrl+C copies, Ctrl+X cuts, Ctrl+V pastes onto the active layer (also on another page), Ctrl+D duplicates, Ctrl+A selects all, Delete removes, Esc deselects. Flip ↔ and Flip ↕ mirror the selection. Everything can be undone. On a Mac use Cmd instead of Ctrl.
  • Undo: Ctrl+Z undoes a step, Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes. It works also after you have touched a slider or menu in the toolbar.
  • Text: the Text menu (between Draw and Edit) is one list (no folds; only Outline, Shadow and Box have a tick box that opens their settings): Type (the Text tool button T, font, size, bold, italic, Weight and Thicken sliders, colour, Text opacity, Letter softness and Fill inside – lower it to make the letters see-through, 0 = only the outline), Layout (align, Wrap width, letter spacing, line height), Outline, Shadow and Box (tick the box to open their settings: outline width, colour, softness and opacity; shadow size; box fill colour, border, softness, opacity and padding) and Fonts and convert (Load font file… uses a .ttf/.otf/.woff from your computer, remembered in this browser; Turn selected text into picture lets you erase, cut or lift parts of it, after which it can no longer be edited as text). Fonts: about 20 ready-made, or Installed font… and type the name of a font already on your computer. Click where the text should go and type; Ctrl+Enter or a click elsewhere finishes, Esc cancels, double-click a text to edit it. While typing you can change any setting and the text updates at once; select a text with the Select tool and the menu shows its settings. Text is a normal object: it lives on a layer and can be moved, resized, rotated, copied and undone.
  • Import images: choose Import image… at the bottom of the Edit menu, drop an image file onto the page, or paste a copied image with Ctrl+V. The picture lands on the active layer and behaves like any object: move, resize and rotate it with the Select tool, flip it, change its layer blend mode and opacity, or rub parts out with the Eraser. With the Select tool, a rectangle or lasso that encloses only part of a picture lifts that part out as its own picture (the rest stays behind with a hole); one that encloses the whole picture, or a single click, selects it all. Large images are scaled down to 1800 px; the editable project file keeps the full picture, while autosave may skip very large images.
  • Cut out: choose ▭ Rectangle or Lasso under Cut out, then draw the shape. Erase inside removes the area, Erase outside keeps only the inside; Soft edge feathers the cut. It works on the active layer (pictures, drawing and text), is non-destructive in the project file and can be undone. Use Layer in the Select tool to move a picture together with its cut.
  • Transform: with something selected, drag a corner square to resize, drag the round handle on top to rotate (hold Shift for 15° steps), or type a Scale % and Rotate ° and press Apply. The Layer button selects the whole active layer so you can transform all of it at once.
  • Layers: under Layers you can add layers, name them, show/hide them, reorder them (↑ ↓) and give each its own Opacity and Blend mode (open a layer’s own menu with the ▸ arrow; the line under the name shows its current opacity and blend mode) (Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Soft light and so on, which mix the layer with the layers below it; they show in the combined export and over a screenshot background, but a PNG placed in RC cannot blend with the page itself). Drawing and the eraser work on the highlighted layer only. In Export, Layers: Each layer as its own PNG gives you one file per layer, handy for placing one over and one under objects in RC. Your browser may ask permission to download several files.
  • Menus: the toolbar has four main menus, Draw, Edit, Layers and Export and save, and each contains smaller menus (Drawing tools, Colour, Thickness and so on). Click any heading to fold it away or open it again; the ⇕ button at the top folds or opens everything. The toolbar remembers what you chose.
  • Presets and colours: under Presets you get one-click pen settings (tool, colour, thickness, pen style). Type a name and press + Save to add your own; ✕ removes one. Under Colour the + button saves the current colour as a swatch (Alt+click a saved swatch to remove it). The toolbar remembers your last tool, colour, thickness, export choices and where you put the panel.
  • Pipette (colour picker): press Pipette (or I) and click anywhere on the screen to pick that colour from the page or your drawing. It shows the colour code (hex and rgb); you can copy it or type your own code. Click the big colour square to choose any colour from the full colour chooser. In Chrome and Edge it picks from anything on screen. In browsers without that feature (Firefox, Safari) it picks from your drawing, or from a screenshot you captured with Capture screenshot.
  • Pressure: 100 is normal; drag right for more sensitivity, left for less; far left gives constant width. Opacity sets how dark the colour is, Contrast darkens light and thin strokes (also strokes already drawn), Stabilise smooths shaky lines. The line “Device: …” shows what your browser reports (pen, mouse, pressure).
  • Screenshot as reference: under Export, press Capture screenshot. The browser asks to share the tab (choose the current tab). The drawing layer and toolbar are hidden for a moment so the screenshot shows only the page. Then choose Background: Screenshot (reference) and Export PNG to get one image with the page behind your strokes. Save reference image saves the screenshot alone. It captures what is visible in the window, so scroll to the right place first. Works best in Chrome and Edge.
  • Where files go: by default exports are saved to Downloads. In Chrome and Edge press Choose folder… once to save directly into a folder of your choice. Chrome refuses some folders (Downloads, your home folder), so make your own subfolder, for example Ritlager. Names are never overwritten; they get -2, -3 and so on. Tick Ask where to save each file for a save dialog every time. Name your files in the File name field. Show image reopens the last export with its own download link. The folder picker does not work on claude.ai-style sandboxed pages, only on normal web pages.
  • Autosave: your drawing is saved automatically for each page in your browser (not in the cloud, not between browsers or computers). Clearing browser data removes it. Clear also removes the saved drawing.
  • Versions: under Save and open, type a name and press Save as new. Open it again with Open, overwrite with Update, start fresh with New blank. Before anything is replaced your current drawing is kept as Previous (auto).
  • Project file: Save project file makes an editable .json file with the whole drawing; Open project file loads it again, and every stroke is still editable. Good for backups and for moving between computers.
  • Underlay: Open PNG… loads an old image at the page’s top-left so you can keep drawing on top of it. Move lets you drag it into place. It is included when you export.
  • If the page grows longer after you started, press Re-measure. Strokes are kept.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. Everything happens in your browser. Free and open source (MIT) at github.com/RutKarin/research-catalogue-tools.
